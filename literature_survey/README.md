- Published **2023–2025**, within the preferred 2023–2026 window
- Drawn from **IEEE, Springer, Elsevier, ACM, Wiley, MDPI** and other
  **Scopus-indexed** venues
- Each citation carries **title, authors, venue, year and DOI or publisher record**
- Each was **verified against the publisher's own record**

## Survey structure

Each paper is assessed against six dimensions, as specified in the Phase-I
guidelines:

| Column | What it records |
|---|---|
| **Paper** | Author, year and venue |
| **Method** | The technical approach taken |
| **Dataset** | Data used for training, validation or review |
| **Advantages** | What the work genuinely contributes |
| **Limitations** | Where it falls short |
| **Research Gap** | What remains unaddressed and why it matters to this project |

Individual gap analyses go further, covering for every paper: **existing methods,
advantages, limitations, research gap and possible improvement**, closing with a
consolidated summary in that student's own words.

---

## Principal finding

The fifteen studies converge on one structural discontinuity: **prediction has
outrun decision, decision has outrun deployment, and platform engineering has
outrun agricultural fit.** Soil-moisture and evapotranspiration models achieve high
accuracy but stop before stating an irrigation depth. Scheduling systems produce
prescriptive advice but only on single instrumented sites. Cloud and federated
architectures scale well but presume compute and sensing that smallholders lack.
Across all three groups, freely available weather forecast data is treated as a
peripheral enhancement rather than the primary driver.

That discontinuity defines the space this project occupies. The full argument is in
the consolidated research gap section of the project report.
